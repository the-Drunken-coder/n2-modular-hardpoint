# P14: bridged receiver roof and flat-print keeper

Current design iteration, 2026-10-29. Goal: improve partial-engagement strength and printability while retaining the simple mini-solenoid mechanism. The current requirement is **0.454 kg carried mass at 2 g**, **8.9 N design load**, and **17.8 N sacrificial proof target** at SF 2; see [requirements.md](requirements.md).

**Evidence status:** CAD and linear stress-screen studies only. No P14 part has been printed or physically tested. The design is not assigned a demonstrated working-load rating.

Recorded CAD boolean checks found no interference between keeper and receiver/adapter at 0, 1.5 and 3 mm travel, or between armature and keeper. Those sampled rigid-geometry checks do not establish as-printed clearance, deflected fit or successful release.

## Straight-pull analysis

Source data: [P14 analysis directory](../project/analysis/p14/fea/) and [selected 1.4/0.6 summary](../project/analysis/p14/fea/B1.4_O0.6/summary.json). Receiver/adapter runs use the frozen linear solver through [n2fea.py](../tools/n2fea.py), with `--prox bridge`; the separate keeper checks move the support definitions to the P14 underside.

The base model is the [effective isotropic PLA screen](../project/analysis/material.json): E = 2200 MPa, ν = 0.35, tension/compression/max-shear thresholds = 25/40/15 MPa. The PAHT-CF values below are **PLA screen crossings multiplied by 47/31**, the assumed ratio of TDS Z tensile strengths. The same factor was applied to compression and shear thresholds without independent validation of those allowables. This is a material sensitivity estimate, **not an anisotropic PAHT-CF simulation or a fracture prediction**.

| Component and modeled state | Scaled stress-screen crossing (N) | Ratio to 17.8 N requirement |
|---|---:|---:|
| Adapter, keeper-floor loading | **33.68** | 1.89× |
| Receiver, both roofs effective | 59.17 | 3.32× |
| Receiver, distal roof ineffective | **36.73** | 2.06× |
| Keeper, both roofs effective | 114.9 | 6.5× |
| Keeper, distal roof ineffective | 71.6 | 4.0× |
| Keeper, half-width contact | 57.4 | 3.2× |

Across these selected straight-pull component checks, the adapter governs at **33.68 N**. Dividing by the chosen SF 2 gives **16.84 N** (16.8 N rounded down), an arithmetic analysis allowance. It is **not a validated working-load rating** and does not replace the **8.9 N design / 17.8 N proof** targets.

### Limits of the result

- Loads act in **+Z, the adapter's extraction direction**. The receiver and adapter are modeled separately with prescribed contact loads; the assembly is not solved as a nonlinear frictional contact/release problem.
- The P14 receiver cases use the `centered_bonded` support definition: **ideal continuous backing**, not the compliance and bearing of the actual two-screw aircraft mounting. The mounting must be tested and, where necessary, modeled.
- The selected summary records receiver mesh size **0.8 mm** and adapter **0.55 mm**. The roughly 7% variation discussed in earlier studies is **not a demonstrated P14 convergence bound**. A P14-specific mesh study remains open.
- No material strength distribution, moisture/temperature degradation, creep, fatigue, wear, impact or full release-path contact model is included.
- Both sampled receiver support states are adapter-governed. This shows improvement in those cases; it does **not** establish that every intermediate keeper position or tolerance combination has unchanged capacity. **R5d remains open.**

The old P13 receiver screen was 18.33 N after material scaling. Alternative bridged geometries reported adapter screens of 30.2 N for lug 1.2 / overlap 0.8 and 25.7 N for lug 1.0 / overlap 1.0; the 1.4 / 0.6 geometry therefore remains selected.

Off-axis loading is outside R3. For context, the selected P14 adapter XZ90 screen is only **4.25 N** after material scaling (2.12 N after SF 2). Socket-wall contact is not included in that baseline, so the result is model-dependent; it cannot be used to infer an installed off-axis rating.

## Back-drive sensitivity check

The existing preliminary calculation assumes horizontal contact, +Z payload force, X keeper motion, and an **unmeasured** friction coefficient μ = 0.2. In an ideal sliding-wedge model, atan(μ) = 11.3° is the friction angle.

The recorded peak-displacement quantities are 0.00183, 0.00119 and 0.00136 mm/N for adapter, distal-ineffective receiver and keeper. Their sum at 8.9 N is 0.039 mm; dividing by an assumed 1 mm contact length gives about 2.2°. An illustrative doubling for stiffness uncertainty gives about 4.5°.

This calculation suggests a testable hypothesis, not proof against back-drive. Sums of component peak displacements do not establish actual relative contact rotation; friction, deformation, tolerance and spring force have not been measured. **R5c remains open.** Check power-off retention and keeper movement under load, then measure release force through the stroke.

## Return-force and vibration sensitivity check

The earlier moving-mass bound uses a 449.5 mm³ keeper at an assumed 1.2 g/cm³ (0.54 g), plus the full listed 12.6 g solenoid mass as an upper bound on its unknown moving armature mass: approximately **13.1 g** total.

For an **illustrative 2 g acceleration along keeper travel**:

```
F_inertia = 0.0131 kg × 2 × 9.81 m/s² ≈ 0.26 N
Illustrative 2× preload target ≈ 0.52 N
```

R2's flight load factor does not define a vibration spectrum, shock severity or resonance response. This calculation is therefore not a vibration pass criterion by itself. Measure return force across the stroke and establish the aircraft's relevant vibration exposure.

The actuator listing's approximately 80 gf (0.78 N) value at 5 V is not a full-stroke, warm-coil force guarantee. Available pull must exceed the measured return force **plus loaded friction and binding** throughout unlocking, with the actual supply/driver. Increasing preload may prevent release. **R5e and loaded-release performance remain open.**

## Next verification gate

Follow the [P14 bench test plan](test-plan.md): print/slicer inspection, material coupons, actual two-screw mounting, measured actuator/keeper stroke and force, unpowered retention, **17.8 N sacrificial proof**, and repeated commanded release at **8.9 N**. Also resolve partial engagement, installation vibration and intended carriage duration.

An unlocked keeper and an adapter that clears are separate outcomes. A hanging-weight bench test does not demonstrate separation of a lifting glider from an aircraft.

# R4 actuation: direct servo drive at nominal 5 V

## Selected prototype

R4 uses **Hitec HS-65MG, manufacturer part 32065S**, driving the loaded keeper through a horn pin and an integral slotted yoke. The keeper still directly retains the payload lug. There is no separate sear, seating interlock or equipment-lock mechanism.

The earlier Adafruit 3992 arrangement remains in the R3 history. It provides advertised travel but its published data did not establish sufficient starting pull at the installed long gap at 5 V; it also accounts for 68 g before the receiver and hardware. The servo is 12.5 g excluding its horn according to the manufacturer datasheet. The user's stated acceptance of a servo permits resolving this design decision without another selection round. This choice provides a buildable prototype interface; its force and thermal behavior remain measured acceptance items.

Manufacturer sources: [HS-65MG product](https://www.hiteccs.com/actuators/product-details/HS-65MG), [HS-65MG v2.2 datasheet](https://www.hiteccs.com/public/uploads/data_sheet/HCS_HS-65MG_Specsheetv2.2_10-1729888021.pdf), [3992 product](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3992). The product page's 11.9 g entry differs from its PDF; the mass budget conservatively uses the PDF's 12.5 g plus separately modeled horn and fasteners.

## Purchased interface and machining operation

Use the **Hitec 55718 Micro 25T horn and hardware set**, or the identical horn supplied with a verified HS-65MG. Use the **MS-I25** engineering-plastic horn, with the manufacturer hub and spline retained. A standard full-size 25T horn has a different spline and is not a substitute. The [Hitec horn drawing collection](https://shop.multiplex-rc.de/userdata/files/HITEC%20SERVO%20ACTUATOR%20HORN%20%26%20SPLINE%20TECHNICAL%20DATA%20SHEET_v3_0_1_26C.pdf), page 10, gives the first existing hole at **7.5 mm radius**. The [55718 product listing](https://hitecrcd.com/hs-65-65mg-5065mg-horn-hdwr/) identifies compatibility.

The mechanical drawing and CAD parameters are authoritative for the final trim, pin stack, yoke, mounting coordinates and travel. Enlarge the selected first horn hole from its nominal 1.0 mm to the drawing's M2 clearance diameter using a supported drill setup. Do not freehand relocate the hole to create a different radius, drill the spline hub, substitute a printed spline, or clamp a threaded screw directly against the moving printed slot. The pin's **HiroSugi CU-2004 SUS303 sleeve** is a fixed sliding follower: nominal 4 mm outside diameter, 2.2 ±0.1 mm bore and 4.0 ±0.1 mm length. It is clamped between the horn and a standard M2 washer/nyloc stack, while the 4.4 mm printed slot moves around its smooth cylindrical surface. This is not a ball bearing. The [manufacturer's CU-series catalogue](https://us.c.misumi-ec.com/book/HSK1_USA_M01/pdf/0154.pdf) defines the purchased spacer; no custom miniature rings are required. The M2×10 fastener clamps the sleeve/horn stack while allowing yoke movement. Inspect for splitting, ovality and adequate remaining edge material. The minimum accepted horn width at the modified hole is 4.5 mm, giving at least 1.2 mm nominal material per side around a 2.1 mm hole; this is an inspection criterion, not a manufacturer strength rating. With the assembled drawing stack, inspect at least0.30 mm free axial clearance between the moving yoke and the outer washer, and at least0.10 mm between the yoke and horn, and check free travel before loading. Reject a sleeve with burrs or sharp edges; deburr only the edges without grinding its diameter to an undocumented size. After repeated releases inspect slot wear and log any increase in measured extraction force. The original Hitec horn screw secures the spline hub and is a separate fastener from the yoke pin.

The manufacturer's CAD link on the HS-65MG product page currently downloads `HS-65HB-1784046298.stp`, whose internal assembly name is HS-65HB. This is a supplier family envelope, cross-checked against the MG dimension sheet. Gear material and internal construction are not inferred from that file. Actual horn seating height, case fit, wire exit and mount-screw engagement are inspected on the purchased sample before printing a load-bearing prototype.

## Kinematics and power states

The servo shaft is horizontal. The crank rotates in the X–Z plane and its pin travels vertically within the keeper yoke while withdrawing the keeper in +X. This avoids the earlier 16 mm transverse pull offset. The yoke and keeper are one physical printed part. The nominal shaft pivot is **X75 mm, Z10.5 mm**, crank radius **7.5 mm**, and retained angle **0°**. The **operating release target is 9.5 mm at approximately 105.466°**. The **10.5 mm / 113.578178°** position is the mechanical travel limit and full CAD clearance envelope, not the commanded operating endpoint. Preserve at least 0.5 mm measured gap to the open stop after calibration.

For a 7.5 mm crank, the ideal displacement from the exactly retained dead-center position is `s = 7.5 (1 − cos θ)` mm. A 9.5 mm operating stroke corresponds to about 105.47°; the 10.5 mm physical envelope corresponds to about 113.58°. For an offset retained angle, subtract the retained crank projection as recorded in the CAD. This relation specifies commanded geometry, not achieved travel under load.

| Physical situation | Intended behavior and verification boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Retained and servo unpowered | The keeper supports the lug. Near-dead-center geometry and the designed stop reduce axial backdrive, but backlash, friction, print deformation and stop contact require a power-off retention test. No certified passive-lock claim is made. |
| Powered release | The servo directly pulls the loaded keeper through its stroke. The force profile, pin bearing, guide friction and current must be measured. |
| Power lost during withdrawal | Position is indeterminate. The servo has no return spring. It may remain partly withdrawn, backdrive or continue through stored energy. The controller does not claim automatic relatching or successful separation. |
| Power restored or controller rebooted | Both power and signal enables remain off. No automatic motion is commanded. A fresh, deliberate preparation cycle is required after the payload is supported and the mechanism inspected. |
| Release completed | Servo power is removed after the command window; the keeper is not automatically returned into a departing payload. Reinstallation is a separate supported operation. |

## Force and torque calculation

At 4.8 V the manufacturer lists 1.8 kgf·cm stall torque and 0.4 kgf·cm peak-efficiency torque: approximately **0.1765 N·m** and **0.03923 N·m**. At a 7.5 mm radius these correspond to ideal tangential equivalents of **23.5 N** and **5.23 N**. Neither number is a guaranteed available keeper force or a continuous rating. Voltage, motor speed, gear friction, linkage geometry, pin-slot friction, acceleration and temperature change the result.

In an ideal frictionless yoke the torque required by axial force is `T = F r sin θ`. Near dead center the ideal mechanical advantage increases, but finite friction, clearance and elastic deflection dominate; the equation must not be used to claim infinite available force. The 12 N axial structural screening case requires at most 0.090 N·m from this geometry before its additional friction. That is a screening demand, not a proven working load.

The previous 5.63 N release calculation used the R3 geometry and assumed friction, preload and return resistance. It is historical context only. R4's actual geometry and selected preload feed its own release-demand calculation. Acceptance compares **measured available and required crank-shaft torque over the complete loaded stroke**, including the point where the distal support ends. The actual horn/sleeve/yoke/guide path is preserved in the demand test. A disconnected axial keeper pull, single endpoint or stall measurement is insufficient.

## Electrical implementation

[The wiring package](../electrical/README.md) defines the exact offboard bench circuit, components, connector pin assignments and software. The mechanism contains the servo and its lead; the Pico and switching/buffer board remain outside the individual hardpoint. This is intentionally not an aircraft power-distribution design.

Supply nominal 5.00 V. Require **at least 4.80 V at the servo terminals while moving**, with a project upper test limit of 5.10 V. A 3 A bench-source capacity provides transient headroom and does not imply servo force. The stock 160 mm, 28 AWG lead contributes voltage drop, so measure at the servo end where practical rather than assuming the source display is the terminal voltage. The prototype firmware commands 50 Hz PWM within the published 900–2100 µs range. Real endpoints must be calibrated unloaded; numerical angle-to-pulse interpolation is only a starting estimate.

## Sample inspection, calibration and force test

1. Record servo/horn part numbers, specimen ID, source CAD revision, print batch, material conditioning and instrument calibration. Measure case, flange holes, shaft location, horn seating, slot width and final stop clearances against the drawing. Photograph the assembled joint and wire route.
2. Before attaching the horn, inspect the circuit with a dummy resistive load and oscilloscope. Confirm power/signal disabled at boot, reset and USB disconnect, correct signal polarity, no pulse outside 900–2100 µs, and switched-rail discharge. Verify both physical slide switch and automatic enable behavior.
3. Support the mechanism with no payload load. Use the explicitly invoked single-command [calibration tool and boot-file procedure](../electrical/README.md#explicit-unloaded-calibration), keeping the normal controller inhibited. Move the unloaded servo to the nominal retained pulse, fit the horn in the nearest workable spline orientation, then adjust in small increments to the defined dead-center/minimum-X crank position. Select a spline index that keeps both endpoints within900–2100 µs. Verify the retained keeper engagement; do not deliberately force it against the closed stop. Pin/slot clearance means keeper-stop contact alone is not proof of the correct crank angle. Set the release pulse for9.5 mm nominal withdrawal, require at least the measured lug-clearance distance plus0.5 mm travel, and preserve at least0.5 mm measured gap to the open stop. Avoid servo internal stops and sustained buzzing. Record both endpoints, achieved travel, stop gaps and current. Only after this record is accepted set `CALIBRATED = True` in the normal bench firmware.
4. As a friction diagnostic, disconnect the servo joint and pull the keeper with an axial force gauge at ≤1 mm/s. Use the actual pads/preload and print finish, with the straight 2 g equivalent load **8.9044 N**. Record the gauge application point and capture breakaway plus continuous force at ≤0.5 mm position spacing. Repeat both directions; measure release separately from reinsertion. This test omits the sleeve's moving force offset and slot-friction reactions, so it is not the complete B03 actuator demand.
5. For B03, preserve the actual horn, sleeve, yoke, guides, installed pads and 2 g equivalent load. Replace the servo drive with a calibrated instrumented input-shaft fixture and measure the torque required through the actual commanded angle/speed profile. Document fixture friction and its uncertainty. Independently measure available servo torque at matching angles, speed and representative output-shaft side loads, with a warm servo and 4.80 V terminal supply; a 5.00 V comparison is additional information. A controlled brake/torque fixture must allow movement; do not obtain a profile by holding repeated long stalls. Retain angle, keeper-position, torque, speed, voltage, temperature and current traces. Use the minimum available and maximum required values in every ≤0.5 mm keeper-position interval, including breakaway/transients. Begin with brief low-load trials and increase load only while current, travel and temperature remain acceptable. Installation-specific side-load/moment profiles remain additional tests; the numerical lateral/CG sensitivity is not their physical validation.
6. Apply the project torque margin at every loaded position: `Tavailable − uTavailable ≥ 1.5 × (Trequired + uTrequired)`. The CSV retains its N-valued field names: enter each measured torque in N·mm divided by the common **7.5 mm** reference radius in `available_pull_N` or `required_pull_N`, and divide its torque uncertainty by the same radius for the uncertainty fields. These are equivalent tangential forces, not axial keeper forces; the common division preserves the exact torque-margin ratio. The [B03 measurement-boundary specification](../analysis/README.md#b03-measurement-boundary) defines the record. If any point fails, the actuator/linkage has not passed, even when another position has surplus torque. Missing instrumentation or readings remain pending. An uncertainty-free nominal comparison or advertised stall torque is not an acceptance measurement.
7. Run the validation matrix's repeated loaded releases. Required full clearance is the actual measured lug-withdrawal distance plus at least 0.5 mm margin; observe payload separation of at least 10 mm within 500 ms from the beginning of commanded motion. Record command-to-clear time, terminal-voltage minimum, peak/RMS current, temperature before/after, achieved travel, accidental release and inspection. The initial development set requires **three specimens × four conditions × 25 releases = 300 releases total**, with zero failures (100 releases per specimen); this is a prototype screen, not a flight-reliability claim.
8. Repeat retained power-off loading and interrupt power at the specified early/mid/late stroke positions with the payload safely captured by a bench fixture. Record the actual result; there is no assumed spring return. A disconnected power wire and a missing PWM signal are separate trials.

Initial project stop conditions are servo external case >45°C before starting a cycle, external case >55°C after a cycle, servo terminal voltage outside 4.80–5.10 V, visible damage, persistent buzzing, incomplete travel or rising extraction force. These temperatures are conservative prototype controls and are not measurements of winding temperature or published duty ratings. One initial trial is a **preparation-and-release pair**: up to 500 ms of commanded motion in each operation, each preceded by 40 ms power settling, with at least 250 ms of released-button quiet time before FIRE. Keep the servo off for at least 10 s after release before the next trial; the firmware enforces that post-release lockout. The pair can therefore contain two closely spaced powered windows, totaling up to 1.08 s. Increase repetition only after thermal measurements support it. The firmware time window and watchdog do not establish a hardware current or thermal limit.

Raw records and the acceptance validator live in [the R4 analysis package](../analysis/). No physical inspection, servo calibration, powered actuation or loaded release has been performed by the software workflow. Its state remains explicitly pending until those measurements are supplied.
